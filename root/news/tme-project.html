Towards Malaria Elimination (TME) Project: Social and Behaviour Change for Malaria Prevention

2024 – Ongoing ROCS Team 6 min read

Malaria remains a major public health challenge in Zambia, particularly among children under five and pregnant women. Through the Towards Malaria Elimination (TME) Project, ROCS implements a Social and Behaviour Change (SBC) intervention that helps communities adopt positive behaviours to prevent malaria and promote early care-seeking.

What ROCS Does

ROCS works closely with District Health Offices (DHO), communities, leaders, and local structures to strengthen behaviour change communication through:

  • Community dialogues and awareness meetings on malaria prevention and early care-seeking
  • Radio programmes and media messaging that promote correct malaria knowledge
  • School-based activities such as debates, quizzes, and health talks
  • Engagement of traditional, religious, and community leaders as malaria champions
  • Training and mentoring of Community Change Agents (CCAs) to lead peer education
  • Development and distribution of locally appropriate IEC materials
  • Support to community-level review meetings

Who Benefits

Direct beneficiaries include:

  • Children under five
  • Pregnant women
  • Adolescents
  • Women and men in targeted communities
  • People with disabilities
  • Traditional and religious leaders
  • Community Change Agents and volunteers

Indirect beneficiaries: Wider communities across the five districts through community engagement.

Focus Areas of Behaviour Change

  • Consistent and correct use of insecticide-treated mosquito nets
  • Acceptance of indoor residual spraying
  • Early care-seeking within 24 hours of fever
  • Increased understanding of how malaria is transmitted
  • Community responsibility in preventing malaria

Results So Far

  • Supporting 102 rural health facilities across the 5 districts
  • 510 Community Change Agents trained and active
  • 100,000+ community members reached with malaria prevention messages
  • 20,000+ school children engaged through school-based awareness activities
  • 100+ traditional leaders actively promoting positive malaria behaviours

Partners

The TME SBC intervention is implemented by Reformed Open Community Schools (ROCS) in partnership with the Churches Health Association of Zambia (CHAZ) and District Health Offices.
